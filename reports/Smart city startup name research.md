# Koina wins the name, Syndeo backs it up

**The best-fitting name for the founder's Europe-wide smart city branding platform is Koina** (KOY-na, from Greek *koina*, "the things held in common", the root of *koine*, the shared language of the Greek world). The runner-up is **Syndeo** (sin-DEH-o, Greek "I connect"). Koina meets the founder's latest brief best. It is a short classical-root name in the same family as "Sfera" and "Kepos". Its meaning is "a common language between city, citizens, companies and machines", which covers communication and humanised technology. It has two open syllables, is easy to say in German, Dutch, Scandinavian and Polish, and the web screening found only one exact-name user, a Vietnamese agritech startup. Syndeo is stronger on digital infrastructure and enterprise weight, but it is longer, it already has a history among US VoIP and IT firms, and it has a mild "sin" echo in Swedish and German. Every name on the earlier smart-prefixed shortlist is either already used in smart city or govtech (Smartpolis, Smartstad), owned by a German company (Smarta), or weak under EU trademark law because the EUIPO treats "smart" as laudatory or descriptive. So "smart city" belongs in the descriptor, not in the name. The suggested lockup is **"Koina — Smart City Narratives"** with the tagline **"Where cities, people and machines speak one language."** One caveat comes before any decision: **no trademark register (EUIPO, TMview, WIPO, DPMA) and no domain WHOIS could be checked in this research**, because the network proxy blocked them. The ranking below is a strong shortlist, not a cleared name.

## The founder's latest brief changes the winning profile

The brief moved in two steps, and the second one has priority. At first the founder wanted the name itself to say "smart city". The latest input asks for a short classical-root name in the style of "Sfera" (used for another of the founder's projects) and "Kepos". That name should evoke human–machine communication, robots, the humanisation of technology, communication, big companies and digital infrastructure, and it should still be energetic, startup-sounding, unique, simple and easy to pronounce. The product sets the other limits. It turns live and curated data from six smart city areas into a daily brand narrative for each city. It is sold both to city governments and municipal marketing offices and to investors and large companies. And it grew out of "Bremen City Pulse".

The competitive landscape supports the classical direction. Among urban-data and govtech companies, the ones with the most money mostly avoid "smart". Hayden AI reports **$178–193M** in total funding ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/hayden-ai-venture-gotech-opengov/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/268302-52)). Quantela has **$50M** ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/quantela/__QD1eggPmc4xh_QPmhzDRE9teooLrEYrfeqOt7ul5-CE)). Berlin's Polyteia, whose name comes from Greek *politeia*, raised a **€5M seed** to sell data analytics to public administrations ([Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/news/polyteia-raised-5-million-in-a-seed-round-backed-by-hv-capital-joined-by-the-german-early-stage-vc-firm-dvh-ventures/)). The pattern repeats in human–machine communication. Berlin's Parloa raised a **$350M Series D at about $3B** ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/parloa-triples-valuation-350m-series-d-general-catalyst)). Hanson Robotics named its robot Sophia because "in Greek, the word Sophia means wisdom" ([Hanson Robotics](https://www.hansonrobotics.com/sophia/%22)). The leaders tend to have 2–3 syllables, an open vowel ending and a root about speech, mind or the common good. Koina has all three.

The buyer side points the same way. Guidance on government branding says public buyers rank **credibility over flash**, because a failed vendor costs agencies "political capital and taxpayer dollars" ([BB Director](https://bbdirector.com/brand-id-and-development/government-contract-branding/)). European city networks sign up to principles that are explicitly citizen-centred and treat "technology as a tool rather than a goal" ([Eurocities](https://eurocities.eu/projects/living-in-eu/)). A real classical word with a civic meaning passes that test better than a playful coinage. Meanwhile, the term "smart city" itself has entered a "post-hype" phase. A 2025 special issue of the *International Journal of Urban Sciences* has the title "Re-examining Digital Urban Infrastructure after the Smart City Hype" ([Taylor & Francis](https://doi.org/10.1080/12265934.2025.2567097)).

## Seven "Smart" names fail on conflicts, generic meaning or trademark law

The decisive problem with the smart-prefixed shortlist is legal, not a matter of taste. EUIPO and General Court practice treats "smart" as laudatory or descriptive. **SMART!** was refused for lack of distinctiveness, and the argument that many "smart" marks were already registered was rejected ([Trademark Lawyer Magazine](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/polish-e-commerce-market-leader-will-not-register-the-mark-smart/)). **SMARTSURFACE** was refused as descriptive in Case T‑463/18 ([EUR-Lex via SDU](https://inenuitvoer.sdu.nl/content/CELEX-62018TJ0463-ENG)). **smart:)things** was invalidated in Case T‑48/19, where the Court held that terms referring to "technologically advanced solutions" should not be monopolised ([EUR-Lex via lagen.nu](https://ferenda.lagen.nu/celex/62019TJ0048)). Any "Smart + city word" mark therefore starts out weak in exactly the classes that matter here (35, 41, 42), before any conflict search.

The names also fail on the merits one by one. **Smartpolis** is the most crowded. It was the name of a Horizon 2020 Budapest smart city centre of excellence ([CORDIS 664605](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/664605/reporting/it)). It is also a "Global Municipal Platform" whose agency portfolio lists Athens as a prospect ([about-smartpolis.com](https://about-smartpolis.com/)), and a Brazilian municipal-services app updated in September 2025 ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.asp.smartpolis&hl=en_US)). In Swedish, "polis" also means police. **Smartopolis** is confusingly close to it: search engines return Smartpolis results for it ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/videos/a/analog-devices/smartopolis)). **Smarta** is the exact name of a Berlin company, Smarta Holding GmbH (HRB 267767 B) ([smartagermany.com](https://www.smartagermany.com/)), and of a UK brand with a US "SMARTA REWARDS" registration in marketing services ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/792/59/smarta-79259880.html)). It also sits next to Swedish *smärta*, "pain". **Smartstad** simply means "smart city" in Swedish (it is the EU's own EuroVoc term ([EuroVoc](https://light.onki.fi/eurovoc/en/page/c_d59e7560?clang=sv))), and Helsingborg already uses it as the name of a municipal project ([Helsingborg](https://media.helsingborg.se/uploads/networks/6/sites/205/2020/12/projektplan-smartstad.pdf)). **Smartburg** is the cleanest of the set, but it is close to Bulgaria's "Smart Burgas" city platform ([Smart Burgas](https://smartburgas.eu/en/faq)), and "Burg" (castle) suggests walls, not openness. **Smartopia** is already the title of a German critical book on digitalisation ([Amazon.de](https://www.amazon.de/Smartopia-Digitalisierung-nachhaltig-Politische-%C3%96kologie/dp/3962380965)) and invites "dystopia" jokes. **SmartCity ID** is purely descriptive, and "ID" reads as digital identity, a field that Estonia's eIDAS-grade Smart-ID already occupies ([smart-id.com](https://www.smart-id.com/)).

The three non-"smart" names do better but miss the new brief. **Genius Loci** is high risk. Its users include the Weimar façade-projection festival ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius_Loci_Weimar)), a Swiss place-promotion platform ([EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/directory/genius-loci/)) and an Interreg-showcased city promotion tool ([Interreg Europe](https://www.interregeurope.eu/community/egle-cruchon)), and the EUIPO refused "GENIUS" alone ([CELEX 62016TJ0425](https://inenuitvoer.sdu.nl/content/CELEX-62016TJ0425-ENG)). **Placeprint** turned up no exact-name user and is low–medium risk, but "print" suggests static printed media, which is wrong for a live-data product. **Cityvane** gets misread as "CityVan", which several European van businesses already use ([share-cityvans.nl](https://share-cityvans.nl/en/)). In Italian, *vane* means "futile" (an observation from general language knowledge).

## Most classical speech words are already taken by AI companies

The obvious Greek and Latin words for speech, mind and connection are mostly gone. **Nous** is an open-source AI lab that reportedly discussed a $1.5B valuation ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/408237/decentralized-ai-project-nous-research-in-talks-to-raise-75m-at-1-5b-valuation-report)). **Noema** is a US computer-vision AI firm ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/noema-1/)). **Loqua** is a voice-agent product ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/loqua)), and the Verba and Glossa families are crowded with AI transcription and translation tools ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/verbaflo-ai/); [indexator.ai](https://indexator.ai/companies/glossa)). **Sermo** is a large physician network ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sermo)). **Ponto** is a Brussels open-banking API ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/company-information/ponto-by-isabel-group)), and **Pontis** is a 120-person software and AI firm with a Düsseldorf office ([The Manifest](https://themanifest.com/company/pontis-technology-doo)). **Omilo** has the right meaning (Modern Greek "I speak"), but it sounds like **Omilia**, an established enterprise conversational-AI vendor that sells to exactly the "big company" buyers this startup targets ([Omilia](https://omilia.com/about-us)). **Hermia** belongs to a Swedish medical-imaging product (per search summary only) ([AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/molecular-imaging/nuclear-medicine/article/15631899/hermes-medical-donates-ai-software-to-regis-college)), and it looks too much like "hernia".

Four names survive with low or medium risk, and each makes a different trade-off.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Koina** | the commons; root of *koine*, a shared language | 2 | Strong / Strong / Medium / Strong | Low–Medium | No literal robot or infrastructure signal; "koi" carp echo |
| **Syndeo** | "I connect, bind together" | 3 | Strong / Medium / Strong / Medium | Medium | Past US VoIP and IT users; Swedish *synd*, German *Sünde* (sin) |
| **Kepos** | garden | 2 | Weak / Strong / Weak / Medium | Low–Medium | Says "garden", not communication; green urban-farming reading |
| **Lalia** | speech, talk | 3 | Strong / Strong / Weak / Weak | Medium | German *lallen* (to babble); sounds childish; near Lala AI, lia.chat |

The only exact-name use of **Koina** found was a Vietnamese food and agritech startup launched in 2021 ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/koina_1/team)), which is a different sector on another continent. **Syndeo** turned up only small, defunct or acquired US IT and VoIP firms ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/syndeo-corporation); [Datanyze](https://www.datanyze.com/companies/syndeo-solutions/476755854)). Because they worked in communications, the overlap with the "communication" theme is real. The only commercial user of **Kepos** is a London point-of-sale reseller ([Endole](https://open.endole.co.uk/insight/brand/231751-kepos-systems)), but Thessaloniki's "Kipos³" community garden, an Interreg good practice ([Interreg Europe](https://interregeurope.eu/good-practices/kipos3)), makes the urban-garden reading stronger. **Lalia** has no exact AI user, but it sits close to Lala AI and lia.chat in conversational AI ([G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/lala-ai); [lia.chat](https://lia.chat/en/about)). The pronunciation and meaning notes in the table come from the researchers' own language knowledge and have not yet been tested with native speakers.

## Koina tells the product's story; Syndeo describes its plumbing

The case for Koina rests on what the product does every day. The platform takes data from six smart city areas and turns it into a narrative that a city government, a marketing office, an investor and a large company can all read. That is literally the creation of a *koine*, a shared language. In the founder's terms, it is the point where machine data becomes human speech, which is the clearest form of "humanisation of technology" a city branding product can claim. It also reads as "the commons", which signals civic legitimacy to public buyers who sign up to citizen-centred principles. At two syllables ending in an open vowel, it is as short as Sfera. A founder portfolio of **Sfera** and **Koina** would hang together naturally: both are classical, both end in "-a", and both describe a shared civic space. The name is also clearly distinct from "City Pulse", which is a common descriptive phrase and would itself be hard to protect under EU trademark law.

Koina does not literally say "robot" or "infrastructure", and no surviving name does. The research found that leaders in social robotics carry warmth and machine identity at the **product or persona** level, with names like Sophia, Pepper and Ameca, rather than in the company name ([SoftBank](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/group/sbm/news/press/2014/20140605_01/); [Wikipedia: Ameca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameca_(robot))). The practical move is to let Koina be the master brand, give each city's daily narrator a persona, and let the visual identity carry the robot and infrastructure cues: a voice waveform, a node network, a dialogue mark. This follows the structure used across place branding, where an ownable master brand sits on top of descriptive product labels, as with Resonance and its "World's Best Cities" ranking ([AAP/Cision](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20251105ae16158)).

Syndeo is the right fallback if the founder decides that enterprise weight and "digital infrastructure" matter more than warmth. "I connect" is the most literal infrastructure meaning on the list, and it sounds like a company that big corporations already buy from. Its weaknesses are real but manageable. It is three syllables, so less punchy. English speakers will say "SIN-dee-oh". The Swedish/German "sin" echo is mild. Its .com, .eu and .de domains point to IP addresses typical of parking services, which suggests they may be for sale (this comes from DNS lookups only, not WHOIS). Kepos should be kept only if the founder's attachment to it outweighs the brief. It carries little legal risk, but "garden" works against every communication, robot and enterprise cue the founder asked for.

**Recommended pairing:**

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Name | **Koina** |
| Descriptor (carries "smart city" and SEO) | **Koina — Smart City Narratives** (for tenders: "Koina Smart City Branding Platform") |
| Tagline | **"Where cities, people and machines speak one language."** |
| Alternative tagline | "Your city's story, told by its data — every day." |
| Origin line | "Born in Bremen as City Pulse." |
| Runner-up lockup | **Syndeo — Connected City Narratives**; tagline "Connecting city data to human stories." |

## Clearance is still entirely ahead of the founder

This research **could not check any trademark register or domain registry**. The egress proxy blocked EUIPO eSearch, TMview, WIPO Global Brand Database and DPMA, and domain status rests only on DNS lookups from a sandbox, where "resolves" means registered and "no DNS" proves nothing. DNS showed that **koina.com, koina.eu, koina.de and koina.io all resolve**, so the exact-match domains are registered by someone. The founder should plan on buying one or using a variant such as koina.city or getkoina.com. Before committing, these steps need to happen, in order. First, run an EUIPO/TMview, WIPO GBD and DPMAregister search for KOINA and SYNDEO, both as exact words and as sound-alikes (Koine, Koin, Coina; Syndeno), in **Nice classes 9, 35, 41 and 42**. Check specifically whether Koina (Vietnam) or any Syndeo firm holds Madrid or EU extensions. Second, check North Data and the German Handelsregister for companies whose trade name is Koina or Syndeo, since German trade-name rights exist even without registration. Third, run WHOIS checks through EURid (.eu), DENIC (.de) and a registrar (.com/.io), and get marketplace prices, especially for the probably parked syndeo domains. Fourth, test pronunciation and connotations with native speakers in German, French, Swedish, Polish, Spanish and Italian. Fifth, check the social handles and the app-store names. Finally, have an IP lawyer give a clearance opinion and file an EUTM, ideally as a word mark plus a figurative mark, before any public launch. If KOINA turns up a blocking mark in class 35 or 42, move to Syndeo and repeat the same steps.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The founder started out asking how to put "smart city" into a name. EU trademark law and the competitive field both say not to: "smart" cannot be owned, and the best-funded peers succeed with ownable classical or coined cores and leave the category words to descriptors. Once "smart city" moves into the descriptor, the name is free to carry the founder's real idea, which is that machine data can become a shared human language for a city. Koina says exactly that in two syllables.

The remaining risk sits in the registers, not in the concept. Classical words are popular trademark material, and every exact-match domain for the top two candidates is already registered. So the founder should treat Koina as the preferred candidate to clear, not as a final name, and budget for a professional search and a domain purchase before anything public carries it.
