# Fresh Brand Name Candidates: Classical-Root Direction (B) and Earlier Smart + City Direction (A)

This file covers new name candidates for the Europe-wide smart city branding platform that grew out of "Bremen City Pulse". The names already considered were not screened again: Smartpolis, Smartopolis, Smarta, Smartstad, Smartburg, Smartopia, SmartCity ID, Placeprint, Cityvane and Genius Loci. Research date: 7 October 2026.

The founder changed the brief mid-task. **Direction B (current)** asks for short classical-root names in the style of "Sfera" and "Kepos". They should evoke human–machine communication, robots, humanising technology, enterprise credibility and digital infrastructure, and still fit smart city branding. **Direction A (superseded)**, smart + city-word names, is kept further down and labelled.

**How the screening worked, and its limits (applies to both directions):**
- **Web search.** Each name got an exact-name web search. A search that finds nothing is weak evidence, because the engine is US-based and small or new EU firms are often not indexed.
- **Trademark registers.** I could not reach EUIPO/TMview (tmdn.org) or WIPO Global Brand Database (branddb.wipo.int); the network proxy blocks them (EGRESS_BLOCKED). DPMA was not tried. **No trademark register was checked**, so every trademark statement is inference.
- **Domains.** I checked .com, .eu, .de and .io with a DNS lookup (`getent hosts`) from the sandbox; the sites themselves could not be fetched.
  - "Resolves" means the domain is registered and pointed somewhere. It may be parked.
  - "None" means the domain is probably unregistered or has no DNS, which is not proven. Registry WHOIS was not checked.
  - Some IPs are typical of parking or marketplace hosts: 13.248.169.48 / 15.197.x / 76.223.x (GoDaddy/AWS accelerator), 91.195.241.232 (Sedo) and 64.190.63.222 (parking). That identification is my own inference.
- **Meanings in other languages** come from my own knowledge of the languages, not from dictionaries. Treat them as inference.

## Direction B: Which classical-root candidates appear free or lightly used, and is "Kepos" itself usable?

### Takeaway
Most real Greek and Latin words for speech, mind or bridge are already taken by AI or software companies:
- Nous (Nous Research)
- Loqua (a voice-agent product)
- VerbaFlo, VerbaAI and Verba
- Glossa (several AI translation products)
- Noema (a computer-vision AI firm)
- Ponto (a Belgian open-banking API)

### Inferences
- **Domains.** Pure classical words are almost always registered on .com/.de, so a short exact-match domain will cost money. Variants like getkoina.com, kepos.city or syndeo.city would probably be needed. Syndeo's .com/.eu/.de look parked (by IP), so they may be purchasable.

### Gaps

- No North Data or Handelsregister check for German companies named Koina, Kepos, Syndeo or Lalia.
- Website content behind the resolving domains was not viewed (proxy blocked).

### Inferences
Pronunciation and meaning notes come from my own linguistic knowledge and were not checked against dictionaries.

- **Koina** (KOY-na, 2 syllables)
  - Pronunciation: easy in German, Dutch, Scandinavian and Polish. French readers may say "ko-ee-NA", and Italian/Spanish readers "KOI-na".
  - Meaning: "the commons / shared things" (cf. Koine, the shared language of the Greek world). It fits a platform that creates a shared narrative between city, citizens and companies, and implies communication through a common language.
  - Language: no negative meanings known in the target languages.
  - Weak on: robots and "smart". "Koi" (the carp) may come to mind.
  - Overall: closest to the "Sfera/Kepos" feel.
- **Kepos** (KEH-pos, 2 syllables)
  - Pronunciation: easy across Europe.
  - Language: no negative meanings known. Spanish "qué pos" is not a real issue.
  - Fit: strong on humanising technology and "city as garden"; weak on communication, robots, enterprise and infrastructure.
  - Overall: the founder already likes it, and legal risk looks low.
- **Syndeo** (sin-DEH-o, 3 syllables)
  - Meaning: "I connect", the best literal fit for communication, network and digital infrastructure, and it sounds enterprise-credible.
  - Language: Swedish/Danish "synd" means sin or "pity" ("det är synd" = "it's a pity"). In German, "Syn-" is pronounced "zün-", close to "Sünde" (sin). The effect is mild but real.
  - Pronunciation: English readers may say "SIN-dee-oh".
- **Lalia** (LAH-lee-a, 3 syllables)
  - Meaning: "speech", directly the human–machine dialogue idea, and it sounds soft and human.
  - Language: German "lallen" means to slur or babble (drunk or baby talk). "Lalia" is also a medical suffix (echolalia). It can sound childish ("la-la"), which works against enterprise credibility. It reads as a woman's first name in Spanish and Italian.
- **Omilo**
  - Fit: good on meaning (Modern Greek "μιλώ", I speak), and Polish "miło" means "nice".
  - Risk: confusion with Omilia, an established enterprise conversational-AI vendor, is too high for a company selling to enterprises.
- **Hermia**
  - Language: also a Shakespeare character. It sits visually close to "hernia", which is negative in English, German, Dutch and the Scandinavian languages.
- **Excluded on conflicts:** Noema, Nous, Verba, Glossa, Loqua, Sermo, Ponto and Pontis. Also, "Nous" is French for "we" (nice meaning, but impossible to search), and German "Glosse" means a satirical column.

### Gaps
- Pronunciation was not tested with native speakers.
- I could not confirm whether Kepos Systems Ltd or Koina (Vietnam) hold EU trademarks.
