# Fresh Brand Name Candidates: Generation and First-Pass Screening

Scope: new name candidates for the Europe-wide smart city branding platform that grew out of "Bremen City Pulse". The names already considered (Smartpolis, Smartopolis, Smarta, Smartstad, Smartburg, Smartopia, SmartCity ID, Placeprint, Cityvane, Genius Loci) were not screened again. Research date: 7 October 2026.

**How the screening worked, and its limits:**
- **Web search.** Each name got an exact-name web search. A search that finds nothing is weak evidence, because the engine is US-based and small or new EU companies are often not indexed.
- **Trademark registers.** I could not reach EUIPO/TMview (tmdn.org), WIPO Global Brand Database (branddb.wipo.int) or DPMA. The network proxy blocks them. **No trademark register was checked**, so every trademark statement below is inference.
- **Domains.** I checked .com, .eu and .de with a DNS lookup (`getent hosts`) from the sandbox. The proxy blocked fetching the candidate sites themselves.
  - A domain that resolves is only registered and pointed somewhere. It may be parked.
  - A domain that does not resolve is probably unregistered, but this is not proven. Registry WHOIS was not checked.
- **Language checks.** The meaning notes come from my own knowledge of the languages. They were not checked against dictionaries in this session, so treat them as inference.

## Which new candidates appear genuinely free or lightly used in the EU smart city / software / marketing space?

### Takeaway
Three names drew no relevant hits: Citysona, Civimark and Cityloom. Citysona and Civimark also had no DNS on any of .com/.eu/.de, which makes them the cleanest. Two names are clearly taken in or near the smart city space and should be dropped: Vivapolis (a French government-backed sustainable-city export brand) and Polispark (a Greek city parking operator with an app). Urbo, which I checked as a control, is also taken by smart city platforms.

### Inferences
- **Probably parked domains.** 15.197.148.33 and 13.248.169.48 are AWS Global Accelerator addresses that GoDaddy commonly uses for parked or forwarded domains. So cityloom.com and polivo.com are probably parked or for sale rather than running businesses. 91.195.241.232 is in a range Sedo uses for parking, so urbanova.eu is probably parked. Not verified: the pages could not be fetched.
- **Brandpolis .com.** The IPv6 prefix 2a01:238 belongs to Strato, a German host. brandpolis.com is probably registered by a German holder, but I could not tell whether it is active.
- **Domain availability by name:**
  - Citysona and Civimark are the only candidates where all three target domains appear unregistered.
  - Brandpolis, Urbeat, Cityloom and Metrospark have .eu and .de apparently free, but the .com would need to be bought or checked.
  - Smartvia and Citymark are fully registered across all three, so they are less attractive.
- **Smart city signal.** None of the free-looking names literally contains "smart". Smartvia is the only screened name that does, and it is the most crowded on domains.

### Gaps
- No EUIPO, DPMA or WIPO trademark search could be done (proxy blocked). This must be run before any decision, at least in Nice classes 35, 41 and 42 (advertising/marketing, information/education, software/SaaS).
- I could not view the content of any resolving domain, so whether it hosts an active business is unknown.
- Not checked: North Data/Handelsregister, LinkedIn, Crunchbase or CORDIS for exact names. The search tool returned aggregator look-alikes only.

Polivo and Smartvia are Medium. Polispark and Vivapolis are High because live businesses or projects already use those exact names in city-related fields.

### Inferences
Pronunciation and meaning notes use my own linguistic knowledge and were not checked against dictionaries.

- **Citysona** (SIT-ee-SOH-na, 4 syllables)
  - Fit: strong on "brand identity / persona", which matches the product's daily image, positioning and identity narratives. "Persona" is familiar in German, English, French, Italian, Spanish, Dutch, Polish and the Scandinavian languages, and "City" is a common loanword (German "City" = town centre).
  - Language: no negative meanings known.
  - Downsides: one syllable over the 2–3 target, and "smart" is only implied. A mild risk that people mishear it as "City Sonar".
- **Civimark** (CHI-vi-mark / TSI-vi-mark / SI-vi-mark, 3 syllables)
  - Pronunciation: the "Civi" opening changes by language (Italian "chi", German "tsi", English/French "si"), so it is less consistent across languages than Citysona.
  - Fit: signals civic/government buyers plus brand. It suits buyers in city governments and marketing offices, but sounds more govtech than energetic.
  - Language: no negative meanings known.
  - Downside: possible confusion with CiviCRM and the "Civi-" family of civic-tech names.
- **Cityloom** (SIT-ee-loom, 3 syllables)
  - Fit: the metaphor of weaving data threads into a narrative suits the product.
  - Pronunciation: "loom" is opaque to many non-English speakers. Dutch and German readers may say "loom" the English way, but French or Spanish readers may say "lo-om".
  - Language: no negative meanings known.
  - Downside: possible association with Atlassian's Loom.
- **Brandpolis** (BRAND-po-lis, 3 syllables)
  - Fit: the closest in spirit to "Smartopolis" and easier to say. The meaning "city brand" is direct.
  - Language: "Brand" means *fire* in German, Dutch, Swedish, Danish and Norwegian. "Polis" means *police* in Swedish (also Turkish), so in Swedish it can read as "fire police". In German, "Brand" in a marketing context is usually read as the English word, but the double meaning will be noticed.
  - Risk: does not signal "smart". Medium.
- **Urbeat** (UR-beat, 2 syllables in English)
  - Fit: energetic and startup-like; keeps the "pulse" heritage.
  - Pronunciation: German readers may split it as "Ur-be-at", and "Ur-" in German means primordial or original. French and Spanish readers may say "ur-be-AT". The spelling is ambiguous.
  - Downside: close to music brands ("Urban Beat").
- **Metrospark** (ME-tro-spark, 3 syllables)
  - Fit: energetic, but "Metro" reads as subway/transit in most EU languages.
  - Trademark: in Germany, METRO AG (wholesale) holds a famous "METRO" mark. This is from my own knowledge and was not checked in a register, but it suggests opposition risk.
- **Polivo** (po-LEE-vo, 3 syllables)
  - Fit: weak "smart city" signal.
  - Language: in Spanish it sits close to "polvo" (dust; vulgar slang for sex). Medium.
- **Smartvia** (SMART-vee-a, 3 syllables)
  - Fit: has "smart", but "via" (road) suggests mobility or navigation rather than branding. Generic.
  - Domains: all three resolve, which suggests existing holders. Medium.
- **Polispark** (POH-lis-park)
  - Already taken (Greek parking operator), and has the Swedish "police" reading. High.
- **Vivapolis**
  - Already taken in the same domain (French sustainable-city brand). High.

### Gaps
- Pronunciation and negative-meaning checks were not tested with native speakers or checked in dictionaries in this session.
- METRO AG's trademark scope was not checked in a register.

## Rank the best 3–5 surviving fresh names

### Takeaway
Recommended order, pending a formal EUIPO/DPMA trademark search:
1. **Citysona** (cleanest; best fit for brand narratives and identity)
2. **Civimark** (clean; speaks to the government buyer)
3. **Cityloom** (clean on search, but the .com is probably parked)
4. **Brandpolis** (keeps the Smartopolis idea, but has the fire/police double meaning)
5. **Urbeat** (energetic, but the spelling and pronunciation are ambiguous)

### Cited Findings
- Citysona and Civimark had no exact-name search hits and no DNS for .com/.eu/.de — [Citysona search look-alike only: City Sonic](https://festivalfinder.eu/festivals/city-sonic-international-sound-arts-festival); [Civimark search look-alike only: CiviCRM](https://civicrm.org/fr/node/6033)
- Vivapolis and Polispark are excluded because live users of the exact name exist in sustainable-city and city-parking contexts — [French Treasury on Vivapolis](https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/Articles/594a05f6-1d7a-45a3-9ba3-fddd3eaee530/files/28e885ac-6b51-432d-944b-b137902d201e); [PolisPark App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gr/app/polispark/id1609932897)

### Inferences
- **Trade-off.** No surviving name has "smart" in it; the clean names get their meaning from "city + brand/persona" instead. If "smart" has to appear, the founder could use a descriptor, e.g. "Citysona – Smart City Branding".
- **Bremen heritage.** Urbeat (pulse/beat) is the only name that keeps the "City Pulse" origin.
- **Next steps:**

  3. Check North Data and Handelsregister for exact-name German firms.
  4. Do a quick native-speaker pronunciation test in German, French, Swedish, Polish and Spanish.
