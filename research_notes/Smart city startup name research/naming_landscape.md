## Q1. Who are the relevant competitors and peers, and what does each do?

### Takeaway
The space has five clusters: (a) city/place-brand indices and consultancies, (b) smart-city indices, (c) urban-data, digital-twin and computer-vision startups, (d) European govtech and data-for-government startups, and (e) EU and municipal programmes whose names set the vocabulary buyers use. No source found a direct "daily data-driven city brand narrative" competitor. The closest peers are the brand indices (they measure perception yearly) and the urban-data platforms (they measure operations live).

## Q2. What naming patterns appear, and which do the most successful use?

### Takeaway
Descriptive "Smart/City/Urban" names dominate **public programmes, indices and small vendors**. Venture-scale companies lean toward **short coined or metaphorical names**, often with a hidden Latin/Greek root (Numina, Polyteia) or a "city-" blend (Cityzenith, Cityblock), sometimes followed by a category tag ("AI").

### Cited Findings
- Hayden AI (person-style name plus "AI") has the largest disclosed funding in the set, at $178–193M depending on source. — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/hayden-ai-venture-gotech-opengov/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/268302-52)
- Quantela (a coined name) has $50M. — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/quantela/__QD1eggPmc4xh_QPmhzDRE9teooLrEYrfeqOt7ul5-CE). Cityzenith (a city- blend) has $11M. — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/cityzenith/__E_lej11a-RB6hCngni_-Y93MeDE30eEjGCNvkvY6ppg/funding-and-investors). Polyteia (a Greek root) has a €5M seed. — [Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/news/polyteia-raised-5-million-in-a-seed-round-backed-by-hv-capital-joined-by-the-german-early-stage-vc-firm-dvh-ventures/)
- Alphabet's urban unit named its products with **short, concrete metaphors**: Pebble, Mesa, Delve, Canopy, Replica. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/12/16/sidewalk-labs-products-will-be-folded-into-google-proper/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk_Labs)
- Resonance's ranking uses alliterative pillar names (**Livability, Lovability, Prosperity**), which shows that place-branding audiences respond to emotive, human words. — [AAP/Cision](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20251105ae16158)

### Inferences
- **Latin/Greek "polis/civitas/urbs" family:** Polyteia is the only clearly funded example found. Many "-polis", "Civi-" and "Urb-" names exist anecdotally (for example civic-tech "Civitas"-type names), but I found no evidence that any of them reached scale. The root signals "city" to educated European buyers but is hard to own.
- **Metaphor names work best when they evoke a felt quality of the city** (pulse, canopy, sidewalk, resonance). That suits a *branding* product better than a *data* product. The startup's origin name, "City Pulse", already sits in this family. Note, though, that "CityPulse" was also the name of an EU FP7 smart-city data-stream research project, background knowledge to be verified.
- **Category suffixes** ("AI", "Labs", "Index") are a cheap way to signal a smart/tech category without the word "smart" (Hayden **AI**, Sidewalk **Labs**).

### Gaps
- I found no systematic dataset (Crunchbase or Dealroom) linking name pattern to funding, so the pattern-to-success link is suggestive, based on about 10 data points.
- I could not confirm the funding or existence of specific "-polis" startups.

## Q3. How crowded are "smart" and "-polis", and is "smart city" dated or rising (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
"Smart city" remains official EU and index vocabulary: the EU Cities Mission, IMD Smart City Index, EC Smart Cities Marketplace. However, academic and trade sources now frame it as "post-hype". Policy language is shifting toward **"digital transformation", "local digital twins", "data spaces", "urban data platforms", "climate-neutral" and "AI"**. "Smart" is crowded and legally weak (see Q5). It works as a *descriptor* but makes a poor *brand core*.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 special issue of the *International Journal of Urban Sciences* is titled "Re-examining Digital Urban Infrastructure **after the Smart City Hype**". — [Taylor & Francis](https://doi.org/10.1080/12265934.2025.2567097)
- Governing: "Despite a shift in the definition of the term 'smart city' in recent years, the effort to make cities smarter continues", and government is "now focused on a new buzzword, artificial intelligence". The same piece cites the National League of Cities saying the term is still a way to refer to integrating emerging technologies into city processes. — [Governing, "The Next Evolution of the Smart City Is Here"](https://www.governing.com/urban/the-next-evolution-of-the-smart-city-is-here) (snippet only)
- Lineage of terms: digital city → ubiquitous city (U-City, South Korea, 2007) → intelligent city → smart city. — [arXiv 2402.09284](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09284)
- Emerging framings include "intelligent urbanism with AI" — [Springer, J. Cloud Computing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13677-023-00569-6) — and "Urban Metaverse" — [arXiv 2503.04729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04729). The "city of data" framing appears too. — [EthicalGEO](https://ethicalgeo.org/a-city-of-data-how-smart-cities-are-the-new-artificial-intelligence-frontier/)
- The term is still heavily used: a 2025 systematic review finds tens of thousands of smart-city publications from 2020 to 2025, and says there is "no single template to define 'smart city'". — [MDPI Smart Cities 8(5):173](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6511/8/5/173)
- **EU policy terms:** the Mission is officially "Climate-Neutral and **Smart** Cities" — [Climate-KIC](https://www.climate-kic.org/spotlight-initiatives/netzerocities/). Living-in.EU uses "the European way of **Digital Transformation**" and focuses on "local digital twins, data spaces and urban data platforms" — [Eurocities](https://eurocities.eu/projects/living-in-eu/); [living-in.eu](https://living-in.eu). The EC portal is still named "**Smart Cities** Marketplace" — [EC](https://smart-cities-marketplace.ec.europa.eu/insights/publications/urban-digital-twins-tool-support-decision-making-operations-and-monitoring)
- The EUIPO has repeatedly treated "smart" as laudatory or descriptive (see Q5), and an applicant's argument that "many smart marks are already registered" was rejected. That is indirect evidence of how crowded the prefix is. — [Trademark Lawyer Magazine, SMART!](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/polish-e-commerce-market-leader-will-not-register-the-mark-smart/) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- "Smart city" is **stable in institutional names** but **dated in startup branding**: post-hype academic framing, AI as the new buzzword, and EU implementation language moving to "digital twins / data spaces". A name built on "Smart…" risks sounding like 2015.
- Signal "smart city" through the **tagline or descriptor** ("the smart city brand platform") instead of the name itself. That keeps SEO and procurement findability while the name stays ownable.
- "-polis" is crowded anecdotally, but I found no quantitative evidence of how crowded.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends or Dealroom count of "smart"- or "-polis"-named companies was obtainable.
- No primary article explicitly declaring "smart city is dated" was found. The evidence is indirect: "post-hype" framing and the AI buzzword shift.

## Q4. What do public-sector buyers respond to in vendor names?

### Takeaway
The available guidance comes from agency and marketing blogs, not procurement research. It consistently says government buyers prioritise **credibility, stability and clarity** over playfulness. That argues for a name that is energetic but serious, and easy to say in a council meeting.

### Cited Findings
- "Government buyers now prioritize credibility over cost savings". Failure costs agencies "political capital and taxpayer dollars". Winning brands rest on "credibility, proven performance, and a clear value proposition", not "flashy logos or catchy taglines". — [BB Director, "Does Your Brand Signal Trust?"](https://bbdirector.com/brand-id-and-development/government-contract-branding/)
- Playful, consumer-style identity ("soft pastels, playful illustrations, and abstract narratives") can read to procurement boards as "risk, impermanence, and a lack of operational security". — [Atin Studio, "The Sovereign Brand"](https://www.atin.studio/post/the-sovereign-brand-strategic-identity-design-for-high-compliance-and-govtech-enterprises) (a vendor's opinion)
- The govtech market has long sales cycles and regulated procurement, and startups tend to specialise in a narrow workflow. — [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/best-govtech-startups)
- Living-in.EU's principles (citizen-centred, "technology as a tool rather than a goal", ethical data, open standards) show the values European city buyers publicly sign up to. — [Eurocities](https://eurocities.eu/projects/living-in-eu/); [living-in.eu](https://living-in.eu)

### Inferences
- For this startup the name has to pass a **two-audience test**: energetic enough for investors and location-choosing businesses, and serious enough for a city CMO or economic development agency to put in a tender. Names that are real words or have clear roots (Pulse, Canopy, Resonance, Polyteia) seem to pass better than cute misspellings.
- Because the product is about city *identity*, a name that sounds European, pronounces cleanly in German, French, Spanish and Italian, and avoids an English-only pun fits the buyers' "European way" framing.
- Tender documents are often in local languages, so a name that is not an English common word also avoids translation or descriptiveness problems.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or procurement-agency guidance (for example EU Public Procurement or a German Vergabe source) on vendor naming specifically.

## Q5. Trademark angle: EUIPO on descriptive and laudatory "smart" marks

### Takeaway
EUIPO and General Court practice treats "smart" (and "smart" plus a product noun) as **laudatory or descriptive under Art. 7(1)(b)/(c) EUTMR**. A mark like "SMART CITY …" for urban data or branding services in classes 35, 42 or 41 would very likely be refused or end up weak. Coined or arbitrary names are the safe path.

### Cited Findings
- **SMART! (Allegro, Polish e-commerce company):** refused under **Art. 7(1)(b) EUTMR 2017/1001** for lack of distinctiveness. "Smart" was read as "intelligent, having a certain degree of intelligence" and as a promotional term. The exclamation mark added nothing, and the argument that many "smart" marks were already registered was rejected. — [Trademark Lawyer Magazine](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/polish-e-commerce-market-leader-will-not-register-the-mark-smart/) (snippet only)
- **SMARTSURFACE (Novartis), Case T-463/18 (General Court, 12 March 2019):** refused under Art. 7(1)(c) (descriptive) and 7(1)(b), and the appeal was dismissed. — [EUR-Lex text via SDU](https://inenuitvoer.sdu.nl/content/CELEX-62018TJ0463-ENG)
- **smart:)things, Case T-48/19 (General Court, 15 October 2020):** the Board of Appeal found SMART THINGS descriptive and the emoticon non-distinctive. It held that "no trademark protection should be allowed for terms which refer to technologically advanced solutions" even when those terms are not yet widely known at filing, and that "smart" with a positive emoticon was laudatory. The Court rejected the argument that "smart" applied only to humans at the filing date. The mark was invalidated at Samsung's request. — [EUR-Lex text via lagen.nu](https://ferenda.lagen.nu/celex/62019TJ0048); [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/trademark/samsung-convinces-euipo-to-invalidate-smart-tm-17049) (snippets only)
- **Principle:** Art. 7(1)(c) exists so that descriptive signs for kind, quality, intended purpose, geographical origin and similar characteristics "may be freely used by all traders". — [General Court case law, lagen.nu](https://lagen.nu/celex/62023TJ0436)

### Inferences
- Owning "Smart City …", "City Data …", "Urban Brand …" or "City Pulse" as plain words would be hard. "City Pulse" is a common descriptive phrase meaning a city's mood or activity.
- Geographic names ("Bremen …") also face Art. 7(1)(c) geographical-origin objections and would limit the Europe-wide scope.
- Coined or blended names (Cityzenith-style, Polyteia-style) or arbitrary metaphors in an unrelated sense register much more easily. If "smart" or "city" appears at all, it works better as an unregistered descriptor next to the distinctive core.

### Gaps
- No EUIPO decision for the exact phrase "smart city" was found. Searching EUIPO eSearch for "SMART CITY" in classes 35, 41 and 42 is the recommended next step.
- The current EUIPO Examination Guidelines (Part B, Section 4, Chapter 4 on descriptive marks, and the laudatory-terms section) could not be opened to quote their examples.
